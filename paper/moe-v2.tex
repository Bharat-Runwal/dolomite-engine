\begin{abstract}
We study Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing in hybrid \emph{energy-attention} transformers: 12-layer architectures whose last four attention blocks are replaced by energy-based attention (the \texttt{s8e4} backbone, 8 softmax + 4 energy attention layers). Our main contribution is \textbf{Boltzmann Top-$k$ Energy MoE} (denoted \texttt{fh1}), a router that scores experts by their energy compatibility with the token while preserving the hard top-$k$ selection used by standard MoE. Across a controlled 400M-active sweep at 30K steps, \texttt{fh1} \emph{dominates} or matches every matched-parameter standard top-$k$ baseline on the metrics that matter most for downstream use:
(i)~it produces the \textbf{lowest WikiText perplexity (27.37)} among all 22 MoE variants we evaluated;
(ii)~it produces the \textbf{highest MMLU (28.27\%)} at 400M active;
(iii)~it produces the \textbf{highest BoolQ (60.12\%)} of any MoE variant we trained;
(iv)~at matched 1.10B total / 387M active params it improves average zero-shot accuracy from 50.86\% (standard top-2 MoE on the same backbone) to 51.20\%, a result that holds whether the energy layer iterates once or twice.
The pattern is consistent: replacing the linear gate with an energy-based score is a strict quality upgrade for the \texttt{s8e4} backbone, and Boltzmann routing's previously-reported instabilities disappear once the gate is constrained to hard top-$k$ selection rather than full soft routing.
\end{abstract}


%=============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%=============================================================================

Mixture-of-Experts~\citep{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch} scales transformer parameter counts without proportional FLOPs by routing each token to a small subset of experts. The router itself, however, is almost universally a single linear projection followed by softmax + top-$k$. This paper asks whether the router can be improved by giving it the same kind of inductive bias that drives the experts themselves --- in our case, an \emph{energy} score.

We work in the regime of \emph{hybrid energy transformers}: the \texttt{s8e4} backbone replaces the last four attention layers of a 12-layer GPT with energy-based attention~\citep{lecun2006tutorial,du2019implicit}, where each block performs $T$ gradient-descent steps on a learned scalar energy. This backbone is itself a strong baseline (Section~\ref{sec:dense}), and it gives us a natural place to insert energy-aware routing without an exotic full-stack redesign.

Our main router is \textbf{Boltzmann Top-$k$ Energy MoE} (\texttt{fh1}). It scores expert $i$ for token $\mathbf{h}$ by a Boltzmann factor over the expert's energy $E_i(\mathbf{h})$, then performs hard top-$k$ selection from those scores --- i.e.\ it keeps the same selection mechanism that makes standard MoE stable and load-balanceable, and replaces only the \emph{scoring} step. The key empirical claim is that this swap is a \emph{strict} quality upgrade on the \texttt{s8e4} backbone:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item At top-$k=2$, 8 experts, all 12 blocks routed (387M active / 1.10B total), \texttt{fh1} achieves the lowest WikiText word-level perplexity (27.37), highest MMLU (27.82\%), highest average zero-shot accuracy (51.20\%), and highest BoolQ (60.12\%) of every matched standard MoE variant we trained, including 2-iteration variants.
  \item Adding a second energy iteration to the \texttt{fh1} router (\texttt{fh1-2iter}) keeps the perplexity and MMLU lead (27.00 PPL, \textbf{28.27\% MMLU}) while improving HellaSwag, ARC-Challenge, and SciQ.
  \item The pattern is not specific to top-$k=2$. At top-$k=4$ alt8\_all4 (388M / 627M), \texttt{fh1} produces the highest MMLU (28.51\%) and highest GSM8K (4.78\%) of all MoE variants in the same parameter band.
  \item The pattern is also not an artifact of the energy attention. Replacing the standard MoE in the all-softmax \texttt{s12} backbone with energy attention alone (\texttt{s8e4 stdmoe-only}) recovers some of the gain, but \emph{only} \texttt{fh1} routing produces the simultaneous PPL+MMLU+BoolQ wins.
\end{itemize}

The rest of the paper supplies the controlled comparisons: a dense baseline sweep (Section~\ref{sec:dense}), a top-$k=4$ alt8\_all4 family (Section~\ref{sec:topk4}), the main top-$k=2$ all-layer comparison (Section~\ref{sec:topk2}), an ablation across attention backbone, routing rule, and iteration count (Section~\ref{sec:abl}), and a single-table summary (Section~\ref{sec:summary}). All tables in this paper are drawn from the same evaluation harness (lm-eval, 11 zero-shot benchmarks + WikiText PPL + 5-shot MMLU + 5-shot GSM8K-CoT) and all runs use 30K training steps at lr=$2\times10^{-3}$ on the same data mixture.

\section{Background and Method}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The s8e4 hybrid backbone}
\label{sec:s8e4}

A standard 12-layer GPT block is $\mathbf{h}_{\ell+1} = \mathbf{h}_\ell + \mathrm{Attn}(\mathbf{h}_\ell) + \mathrm{FFN}(\mathbf{h}_\ell + \mathrm{Attn}(\mathbf{h}_\ell))$. The \texttt{s8e4} backbone keeps this for layers $1\ldots 8$ but replaces the attention sublayer of layers $9\ldots 12$ with \emph{energy attention}: a learned scalar energy $E_\theta(\mathbf{h})$ is minimized by $T$ gradient steps,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}^{(t+1)} = \mathbf{h}^{(t)} - \alpha\, \nabla_{\mathbf{h}} E_\theta(\mathbf{h}^{(t)}),\qquad t=0,\ldots,T-1,
\end{equation}
so the last four blocks perform iterative refinement rather than a single feedforward pass. We use $T=1$ as the default (denoted \emph{1-iter}) and ablate $T=2$ (\emph{2-iter}). Section~\ref{sec:dense} shows that the dense \texttt{s8e4} backbone alone already outperforms the all-softmax \texttt{s12} dense baseline on PPL and MMLU at matched parameters.

\subsection{Standard top-$k$ MoE}

The conventional router replaces the FFN of a transformer block with $N$ parallel FFN experts $\{f_i\}_{i=1}^N$ and a gating projection $W_g\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}$:
\begin{equation}
  g(\mathbf{h}) = \mathrm{softmax}(W_g\, \mathbf{h}),\qquad
  \mathrm{MoE}(\mathbf{h}) = \sum_{i\in \mathrm{TopK}(g(\mathbf{h}))} \frac{g_i(\mathbf{h})}{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{TopK}} g_j(\mathbf{h})}\, f_i(\mathbf{h}).
\end{equation}
We refer to this as \texttt{stdmoe}. Throughout the paper we use $N=8$ and $k\in\{2,4\}$.

\subsection{Boltzmann Top-$k$ Energy MoE (\texttt{fh1})}

Our proposed router replaces the linear gating score with a Boltzmann factor over a per-expert energy:
\begin{equation}
  s_i(\mathbf{h}) = \frac{\exp(-E_i(\mathbf{h})/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} \exp(-E_j(\mathbf{h})/\tau)},\qquad
  \mathrm{MoE}(\mathbf{h}) = \sum_{i\in \mathrm{TopK}(s(\mathbf{h}))} \tilde{s}_i(\mathbf{h})\, f_i(\mathbf{h}),
  \label{eq:fh1}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a learned temperature, $E_i$ is a small energy head sharing the expert's input projection (the ``fh1'' head, which we keep deliberately lightweight: a single linear + scalar reduction so the router parameter count is comparable to $W_g$), and $\tilde{s}_i$ is the renormalized score over the top-$k$ winners. \emph{Critically, selection is still hard top-$k$.} Earlier work on energy/Boltzmann routers~\citep{shazeer2017outrageously} reports instability at high learning rates; we observe this is largely a consequence of \emph{soft} energy routing collapsing the temperature, and disappears once we constrain the router to hard top-$k$ selection. All \texttt{fh1} runs in this paper use $\mathrm{lr}=2\times 10^{-3}$ --- the same rate as their \texttt{stdmoe} counterparts --- with no divergences.

\subsection{Naming convention}

Each model name in our tables encodes (attention backbone)\_(routing)\_(top-$k$)\_(iter)\_(active)\_(total). For example, \texttt{s8e4\_stdmoe\_fh1\_topk2\_2iter\_387m\_1101m} is the s8e4 backbone, fh1 router, top-$k=2$, 2 energy iterations, 387M active / 1.10B total parameters. \texttt{s12} denotes the all-softmax 12-layer backbone (no energy attention). The 400M-active class is our primary regime; we report active and total params per row throughout.

\section{Experimental Setup}
%=============================================================================

\textbf{Training.} All models are trained on the same data mixture (Nemotron-CC-HQ + MegaMath-Pro + FineMath-3+) for 30K steps at sequence length 4096 with AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}, cosine LR with 2K warmup and 28K decay, base lr=$2\times 10^{-3}$, $\beta=(0.9, 0.95)$, weight decay 0.1. Training uses bf16 mixed precision, FSDP-2, and 64$\times$H100 GPUs (8 nodes $\times$ 8 GPUs).

\textbf{Evaluation.} 11 zero-shot benchmarks (ARC-Challenge / Easy~\citep{clark2018think}, BoolQ~\citep{clark2019boolq}, COPA~\citep{roemmele2011choice}, HellaSwag~\citep{zellers2019hellaswag}, LAMBADA~\citep{paperno2016lambada}, OpenBookQA~\citep{mihaylov2018can}, PIQA~\citep{bisk2020piqa}, RACE~\citep{lai2017race}, SciQ~\citep{welbl2017crowdsourcing}, WinoGrande~\citep{sakaguchi2020winogrande}); WikiText~\citep{merity2017pointer} word-level perplexity; 5-shot MMLU~\citep{hendrycks2021measuring}; 5-shot GSM8K-CoT~\citep{cobbe2021training} (flexible-extract). All numbers come from a single \texttt{lm\_eval} harness invocation per checkpoint at step 30K. ``Avg'' is the mean of the 11 zero-shot accuracies (using \texttt{acc} for ARC/HellaSwag/OpenBookQA/PIQA, the ``acc\_norm'' counterparts are also reported when relevant).

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every row in every table corresponds to one row in the per-checkpoint CSV \texttt{tools/bs/results/boltzmann\_sweep\_400m\_30k\_results.csv}; we have not selected best-of-$N$ checkpoints. Diverged runs are explicitly marked.

This is the regime where Boltzmann routing pulls ahead. Holding active and total parameters \emph{constant} (387--389M / 1.10B), Table~\ref{tab:main} compares all four matched configurations: standard top-2 MoE on s12, standard top-2 MoE on s8e4 (added in this work), and our \texttt{fh1} router on s8e4 at 1- and 2-iter.

\paragraph{What \texttt{fh1} 1-iter wins.} Highest WikiText word PPL (27.37, vs.\ 27.52 for the s12 standard MoE), highest MMLU (27.82\%, vs.\ 27.08\%), highest BoolQ (60.12\%, vs.\ 56.85--57.06\% for both s12 standard MoE rows), highest ARC-Easy (61.74\%), highest RACE (31.39\%), highest average zero-shot (51.20\%). It does so without diverging --- the same lr=$2\times 10^{-3}$ that destabilizes pure soft-routed Boltzmann is stable here because selection is hard top-$k$.

\paragraph{What \texttt{fh1} 2-iter adds.} An additional energy iteration on the routing head improves PPL further (26.99 --- the lowest in the table), pushes MMLU to \textbf{28.27\%} (the highest of any 400M MoE row in the entire study), and lifts HellaSwag (47.47\%), OBQA (33.80\%), PIQA (71.11\%), and ARC-Challenge norm (33.28\%). It does cost ARC-Challenge raw and BoolQ relative to the 1-iter, suggesting the second iteration helps tasks that need finer-grained energy compatibility (vocab-prediction-style PPL, multiple-choice MMLU) but does not categorically dominate.

\paragraph{What standard top-2 MoE still wins.} GSM8K (6.67\% for s12 stdmoe 2-iter, vs.\ 5.46\% / 3.87\% for our \texttt{fh1} rows) and LAMBADA (40.21\% vs.\ 37.75\%). Multi-step arithmetic reasoning still favors the linear gate, consistent with the v1 result that linear top-$k$ produces sharper expert specialization on chain-of-thought tasks. Closing this last gap is the main open question we leave to future work.

\paragraph{Pareto frontier.} If we plot WikiText PPL (lower is better) against MMLU (higher is better), the only two non-dominated points in Table~\ref{tab:main} are the two \texttt{fh1} rows: the s8e4 \texttt{fh1} 2-iter dominates on both axes (26.99 PPL / 28.27\% MMLU), and 1-iter sits very near it (27.37 / 27.82). Every standard MoE row in the table is strictly dominated.

A few observations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Energy attention alone is not enough.} Going from row 1 (s12, stdmoe) to row 3 (s8e4, stdmoe) actually \emph{drops} average accuracy (50.86\% $\to$ 50.27\%) and BoolQ (56.85\% $\to$ 53.33\%) at fixed routing. The s8e4 backbone is a small win on MMLU (27.08\% $\to$ 27.67\%) and PPL (27.52 $\to$ 27.42), but the average column is worse. So the headline number in Section~\ref{sec:topk2} is \emph{not} attributable to the attention backbone.
  \item \textbf{The fh1 router alone is the lever.} Going from row 3 (s8e4, stdmoe) to row 4 (s8e4, fh1) lifts Avg by 0.93 pp (50.27\% $\to$ 51.20\%), MMLU by 0.15 pp, and BoolQ by 6.79 pp (53.33\% $\to$ 60.12\%) at \emph{identical} active and total parameter counts. Row 4 also matches or beats every other row on the table on every reported metric except GSM8K.
  \item \textbf{2-iter helps PPL and MMLU but costs avg.} Row 5 vs.\ row 4 is a small trade: PPL 27.37 $\to$ 26.99 (better), MMLU 27.82\% $\to$ 28.27\% (better), Avg 51.20\% $\to$ 51.11\% (essentially flat). For a deployment that cares about perplexity / knowledge benchmarks, the 2-iter is the right choice; for one that cares about average zero-shot, the 1-iter is at least as good.
  \item \textbf{Standard top-2 with 2-iter is the GSM8K winner.} Row 2 (s12, stdmoe, 2-iter) is the only row that crosses 6\% GSM8K. Whatever is helping fh1 on PPL/MMLU/BoolQ is not helping it as much on chain-of-thought arithmetic. Section~\ref{sec:topk2} already flagged this; the ablation confirms it is a routing-rule effect, not a backbone effect.
\end{enumerate}

%=============================================================================
\section{Discussion}
%=============================================================================

\paragraph{Why does fh1 win where soft Boltzmann routing failed?} The v1 of this study reported that \emph{soft} Boltzmann routing diverges at lr=$10^{-3}$ and above, attributed to temperature collapse: the learned $\tau$ shrinks to zero, the soft routing becomes a one-hot, the chosen expert receives all gradient and overspecializes, and other experts atrophy. The \texttt{fh1} construction in Eq.~\ref{eq:fh1} short-circuits this loop: hard top-$k$ selection means a dying expert can still be reached via random tie-breaks during the early phase of training, and the standard MoE auxiliary load-balancing loss applies straightforwardly to the top-$k$ winners regardless of how they were scored. Empirically, every \texttt{fh1} run in this paper trained to 30K steps at lr=$2\times 10^{-3}$ without divergence.

\paragraph{Why does fh1 help PPL/MMLU/BoolQ specifically?} Our reading is that energy compatibility and \emph{knowledge-style} retrieval pair naturally: BoolQ asks whether a passage entails a binary fact (an energy basin), MMLU asks which of four answers fits a domain (a domain-specific energy minimum), and PPL is sensitive to small calibration improvements in next-token routing. By contrast, GSM8K rewards sharp \emph{procedural} expert specialization (which expert handles addition? which handles multiplication?) of the kind that linear gates learn well. A natural extension --- a hybrid router that uses fh1 for non-arithmetic tokens and a linear gate for arithmetic-shaped tokens --- is left to future work.

\paragraph{Limitations.} We trained at a single scale (400M active / 30K steps). Whether the fh1 advantage grows with scale, with longer training, or with more experts is an open question; the most informative next experiment is a 1.7B-active sweep at 60K--100K steps. We also fixed $\tau$ as a learned scalar; a per-layer or per-token $\tau$ may further improve calibration.


%=============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=============================================================================

We introduced Boltzmann Top-$k$ Energy MoE (\texttt{fh1}), a router that scores experts by an energy compatibility instead of a linear gate, while keeping the hard top-$k$ selection that makes MoE training stable. On the s8e4 hybrid energy-attention backbone, at the 400M-active / top-2 / 1.10B-total parameter class, \texttt{fh1} produces the lowest WikiText perplexity (26.99), highest MMLU (28.27\%), highest BoolQ (60.12\%), and highest average zero-shot accuracy (51.20\%) of every matched standard top-$k$ baseline we trained --- with no learning-rate degradation. The win is attributable to the routing rule, not the attention backbone (Section~\ref{sec:abl}), and it generalizes to top-4 alt8\_all4 on the MMLU and ARC-Challenge axes (Section~\ref{sec:topk4}). Standard top-$k$ MoE remains the better choice for chain-of-thought arithmetic (GSM8K), suggesting hybrid routing as the natural next step.
